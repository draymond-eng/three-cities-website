# Three Cities Social, Website Copy Brief

**For:** Web design team
**Goal:** Make the site, and the homepage in particular, the strongest result for "social club in Chicago" and the supporting terms we already rank for on Maps. Includes two new standalone neighborhood pages. The copy here goes onto the pages as written.

---

## How to use this

Each section below is one page. For every page you get the target search term, the title tag, the meta description, the H1, the H2 headings, and the body copy to work in. The copy does not replace the design. It fills the sections with searchable language, because right now the copy is so minimal that Google has little to rank us on.

Paste the copy as written. Do not rewrite it to sound more polished. It is already in our voice.

---

## Copy rules, do not break these

- No em dashes anywhere. Use periods or commas. This includes title tag separators. Use a pipe, not " — ".
- Connection always leads. Coworking is a feature we mention, never the headline.
- Say what we are, not what we are not. Affirmative framing only.
- No puns, no clever wordplay headers, no corporate filler, no AI-sounding copy.
- Never use the word "salon."
- Never mention any membership cap or limit in public copy.

---

## Navigation menu

Turn the "Locations" item in the top navigation into a dropdown.

- The "Locations" label stays clickable and goes to /locations, the hub page.
- Hovering or tapping it opens a dropdown with two items:
  - River North, links to /river-north
  - Wicker Park, links to /wicker-park
- On mobile, the same two items appear nested under Locations in the menu.

Why it matters beyond the menu: these dropdown links sit on every page of the site, so they act as sitewide internal links pointing at the two new neighborhood pages. That is exactly the link signal those pages need to start ranking, so this one change helps navigation and SEO at the same time.

## Duplicate content, the rule that makes these two pages work

Google penalizes near-identical location pages. To stay clear of that, the River North and Wicker Park pages must each have:

- That clubhouse's own photos, never shared between the two
- Its real amenities and layout, written out, not a generic list
- Its own address, embedded map, and directions
- At least one member quote tied to that location, if available

If the body of the two pages ends up more than roughly half identical, rework them until they are not. The neighborhood voice in the copy above is a starting point, the real amenities and photos are what make each page genuinely its own.

## Internal linking, the anchor text is copy too

The words inside a link are a ranking signal, so they need to match the term each page is trying to win. Use these exact anchors.

- Footer, under Programming: link **networking events in Chicago** to /networking-events-chicago
- Homepage body: link **social club in River North** to /river-north and **social club in Wicker Park** to /wicker-park
- Locations hub: link both neighborhood blocks to /river-north and /wicker-park
- From the new neighborhood pages and the networking page: link back to the homepage using the anchor **Chicago social club**
- In each Google Business Profile, set the website link to the matching neighborhood page, River North profile to /river-north, Wicker Park profile to /wicker-park

The networking page is currently orphaned. Linking to it from the footer and one body spot is a high-impact item in this brief.
